Logo File Formats Every Business Should Have

Your logo will appear on websites, social profiles, invoices, signs, T-shirts and packaging. Each of those uses asks for different files, and scrambling for the "right version" every time wastes hours. A complete logo kit solves the problem once. Here is what to include.

1. Vector master files

These are the most important files you own. They can be scaled to any size without losing quality and edited easily.

  • SVG — for websites, apps, cutting machines and most modern design tools.
  • PDF — for printers and anyone who needs a universally openable vector.
  • EPS — for older print workflows and some sign makers and stock sites.
  • Native source file (AI, Affinity, Figma) — for future design changes.

If you do not have vector files, ask your original designer. If that is impossible, you can recreate a vector version by tracing a high-quality PNG with our PNG to SVG converter and cleaning it up. See How to Convert a Logo to SVG.

2. Raster exports

For platforms that do not accept vectors:

  • PNG with transparent background — the most versatile raster logo. Provide several sizes, for example 500 px, 1000 px and 2000 px wide.
  • JPG on white — for systems that do not handle transparency, such as some document templates.
  • Square profile image — your symbol centred on a solid background, at around 800 × 800 px or larger.
  • Favicon — an SVG favicon plus PNG fallbacks. See How to Create an SVG Favicon.

3. Colour variations

Your logo must work on light backgrounds, dark backgrounds and in single-colour printing:

  • Full colour — the primary version.
  • Reversed / white — for dark backgrounds and photos.
  • Black (one colour) — for faxes, stamps, engraving and single-colour printing.
  • Single brand colour — useful for embroidery, screen printing and promotional items.

4. Layout variations

  • Primary (horizontal) — symbol and wordmark side by side.
  • Stacked — symbol above wordmark, for square spaces.
  • Symbol only — for small spaces like app icons, social avatars and favicons.
  • Wordmark only — when the symbol appears elsewhere or is too detailed for the space.

5. Brand information

Include a short one-page guide with:

  • Colour values in HEX (web), RGB (screen), CMYK (print) and Pantone (if used)
  • Font names
  • Minimum size and clear space around the logo
  • Examples of incorrect use (stretching, recolouring, adding effects)

Suggested folder structure

logo/
  vector/   logo-primary.svg, .pdf, .eps
  png/      logo-primary-1000.png, logo-white-1000.png ...
  social/   profile-800.png, cover-1500x500.png
  favicon/  favicon.svg, favicon-32.png, apple-touch-icon.png
  brand-guide.pdf

Use clear, consistent file names that describe the variant, colour and size.

Commercial printerPDF (CMYK if requested)

Summary

A complete logo kit includes vector masters (SVG, PDF, EPS, source), raster exports (transparent PNGs, JPG, profile and favicon), colour and layout variations, and a brief brand guide. Build it once and you will always have the right file ready.
