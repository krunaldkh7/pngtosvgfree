It happens all the time: a business needs its logo for a sign, a T-shirt or a new website, but the only copy anyone can find is a small PNG pulled from an old email. The original vector file is gone. The good news is that you can usually recreate a clean SVG version in minutes with image tracing. Here is the process we recommend.

Step 1: Find the best source image

Tracing quality depends heavily on the input. Look for the largest version of the logo you can find, ideally at least 500 pixels wide. Avoid screenshots of screenshots and heavily compressed JPGs, because compression creates blotchy artifacts around edges that the tracer may interpret as extra shapes. A PNG with a transparent background is ideal.

Step 2: Upload it to the converter

Open our PNG to SVG converter and drop the image into the upload area, or paste it with Ctrl+V. The tool analyses the image and automatically selects the Logo / Clip art type for flat-colour graphics. This preset limits the number of colours and keeps corners sharp.

Step 3: Check the colour palette

Logos usually contain only a few colours. Look at the palette panel: if you see extra in-between shades, choose a simpler option from Suggested palettes — for example the 2- or 3-colour version. Fewer colours mean cleaner shapes and a smaller file.

You can also click any colour swatch to replace it with your exact brand colour. If the logo sits on a white box you do not want, click the × on the white swatch to make that colour transparent.

Step 4: Fine-tune detail and corners

Open Advanced settings if you need more control:

  • Detail – higher values keep small features such as thin lines and small text.
  • Speckle removal – increase it to remove tiny stray dots caused by noise.
  • Corner angle – lower values keep corners sharp; higher values round them into curves.
  • Noise smoothing – useful for blurry or grainy source images.

Use the compare slider and zoom to inspect edges closely before downloading.

Step 5: Download and review

Click Download SVG. Open the file in Figma, Illustrator or Inkscape and check that each colour sits on its own layer. Tracing is an approximation, so very small text may still need manual touch-ups. For text-heavy logos, consider retyping the wording with the original font for perfect results.

Good to know: Tracing works best with flat colours. Logos containing gradients, shadows or photographic textures will be simplified into flat colour bands.

Common problems and fixes

  • Jagged edges – use a larger source image or raise noise smoothing by one step.
  • Too many colours – pick a suggested palette with fewer colours.
  • Missing thin lines – raise Detail and lower Speckle removal.
  • White halo around shapes – make the white colour transparent or reduce the colour count.

With a good source and a few tweaks, you will have a scalable, editable logo ready for signs, merchandise and the web.
