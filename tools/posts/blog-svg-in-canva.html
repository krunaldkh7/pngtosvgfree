Canva is a favourite tool for social media graphics, presentations and printables. Uploading your own SVG files — logos, icons and illustrations — keeps them sharp at any size and, in many cases, lets you recolour them right inside Canva. Here is how to get the best results.

Uploading an SVG

  1. Open a design and click Uploads in the left panel.
  2. Click Upload files and choose your .svg file, or drag it into the panel.
  3. Click the uploaded item to add it to your design.

SVG upload has been available to all Canva users for some time, but features can change between plans and platforms — if the option is missing, check Canva's help centre for current support.

Recolouring an SVG

Select the SVG in your design. If Canva can recolour it, colour swatches appear in the top toolbar — one for each colour in the file. Click a swatch and choose a new colour or enter a hex code. This is a great way to match an icon or illustration to your brand colours.

Canva limits how many colours it shows for recolouring. SVGs with many colours — for example traced photographs — may display only some swatches or none at all. Simple files with a handful of flat fills work best.

Preparing SVGs for Canva

  • Keep colours few and flat. When tracing, set a low colour count. Our PNG to SVG converter lets you choose exactly how many colours to keep.
  • Remove the background so the graphic sits cleanly on any design.
  • Convert text to outlines. Canva cannot use fonts embedded in an SVG; outlined text looks correct everywhere.
  • Avoid complex effects. Filters, masks and some gradients may not display as expected. Flatten or simplify them in a vector editor first.
  • Keep file size reasonable. Extremely complex SVGs can upload slowly or fail. Simplify paths if needed.

Resizing without quality loss

Because SVG is vector, you can stretch it across a full poster and it will stay crisp — unlike PNG logos, which blur when enlarged. Hold Shift while dragging a corner handle if you need to resize without keeping proportions.

Exporting your design

Canva exports whole designs as PNG, JPG, PDF and, on some plans, SVG. For print, choose PDF Print to keep vector elements sharp. For the web, PNG or JPG are usually fine. If you need an SVG export, check whether your plan includes it.

Using Canva designs on cutting machines

Many crafters design in Canva and cut on a Cricut or Silhouette. If your plan cannot export SVG, export a high-resolution PNG with a transparent background, then convert it to SVG with our converter, choosing the Silhouette or Logo type. See Preparing SVG Files for Cricut and Silhouette for cutting tips.

Troubleshooting

  • "Upload failed": the file may be too complex, contain unsupported elements or not be a valid SVG. Open it in a browser to check it displays, then simplify it in a vector editor.
  • No colour swatches appear: the SVG may have too many colours or use gradients. Reduce colours and use flat fills.
  • Parts are missing: masks, clip paths or linked images may not be supported. Expand or flatten them.
  • Text looks different: convert text to paths before uploading.

Summary

Upload SVGs through the Uploads panel, recolour them with the toolbar swatches, and keep files simple: few flat colours, no background, outlined text. Your logos and illustrations will then stay crisp and on-brand in every Canva design.
