What does a JPEG to SVG converter do?

A JPEG to SVG converter traces the pixels of a JPEG image and rebuilds them as vector shapes, producing an SVG file that can be scaled, edited and cut without losing quality. JPEG files are great for sharing photos, but they have a fixed resolution and cannot be recoloured or resized cleanly. An SVG describes the same image as paths and fills, so it stays sharp from a business card to a billboard.

How many colours should a JPEG SVG have?

Use as many colours as a person would name when looking at the image — usually 2 to 6 for logos and 12 to 24 for photos. Every extra colour adds more shapes and a bigger file, while too few colours merges details. Tips for better JPEG tracing

  • Start large. A 1,000-pixel JPEG gives far smoother curves than a 200-pixel thumbnail.
  • Avoid re-saved JPEGs. Each save adds more compression artefacts; use the original file if you can.
  • Crop first. Removing unrelated parts of the image gives the palette detection a cleaner start.
  • Boost contrast for scans. Faded prints and pencil drawings trace better after a simple contrast increase.
  • Use Medium or Low quality for simpler output. Lower quality presets produce fewer nodes and smaller files.
