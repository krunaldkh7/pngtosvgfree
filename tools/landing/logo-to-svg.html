Why do you need your logo as an SVG?

An SVG logo stays perfectly sharp at every size, has a tiny file size, can be recoloured in seconds and is the format printers, sign makers, web developers and cutting machines ask for. Many businesses only have their logo as a small PNG or JPG from an old website or email signature. That file blurs on large prints and high-resolution screens. Converting it to SVG gives you a master file that works everywhere.

How do I convert my logo to SVG?

  1. Upload the best logo file you have

    Use the largest, sharpest PNG you can find — PNG is better than JPG because it has no compression noise.

  2. Set the colours to match the logo

    Logo mode is pre-selected. Set the number of colours to the colours in your logo, including the background if there is one.

  3. Fix colours and remove the background

    Click each palette colour and enter your exact brand hex codes, then click × on the background to make it transparent.

  4. Download and check

    Zoom in on corners and small text in the split view, then download the SVG.

Can I get my exact brand colours?

Yes — click any colour in the palette and type the exact hex value from your brand guidelines before downloading. Tracing averages colours from the pixels, so a brand blue might come out a shade off. Replacing it with the exact value makes the SVG match your brand perfectly, and every path using that colour updates at once.

How do I make the logo text perfect?

For the cleanest lettering, retype the text with the original font in a vector editor and replace the traced letters. Tracing handles most logos very well, but tiny or thin letters from a low-resolution source can look slightly soft. If you know the font, open the SVG in Inkscape, Figma or Illustrator, type the text, match the size and convert it to outlines. Our guide How to Convert a Logo to SVG walks through this.

Why does my traced logo have a white box behind it?

The background of your PNG or JPG was traced as a colour. Click the × on that colour in the palette to remove it and get a transparent background.

My logo has a gradient. Will it work?

Gradients become bands of flat colour. Increase the colour count for smoother bands, or replace them with a real SVG gradient in a vector editor afterwards.
