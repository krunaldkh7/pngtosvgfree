Why does Cricut need SVG instead of PNG?

Cricut machines cut along vector paths, so an SVG gives Cricut Design Space clean, separate cut lines for every colour, while a PNG must be traced and usually cuts as one flattened shape. When you upload a PNG, Design Space treats it as a single-layer image: you can remove its background, but you cannot cut each colour from a different vinyl. An SVG keeps every colour as its own layer, with smooth edges that cut and weed cleanly.

How do I convert a PNG to an SVG for Cricut?

  1. Upload your PNG

    Drop your design into the converter above. Logo mode is pre-selected because it gives the crisp edges cut files need.

  2. Choose how many layers you want

    Set the colour count to the number of vinyl or cardstock colours you plan to use — each colour becomes one Cricut layer. Choose Silhouette for a single-colour cut.

  3. Clean it up

    Raise speckle removal so tiny pieces do not become impossible-to-weed cuts, and click × on the background colour to delete it.

  4. Download and upload to Design Space

    Download the SVG, then in Cricut Design Space choose Upload → Browse and select the file. Each colour appears as a separate layer.

How do I keep layers lined up?

Turn on Registration marks under Advanced settings — it adds four corner marks to every colour layer — then cut them with each layer and use them to line layers up when applying. Registration marks are small squares added at the same position in every colour layer. Alternatively, in Design Space select all layers and use Attach so they keep their positions on the mat.

Cricut tips after uploading

  • Check the size. Design Space may import SVGs at a different size from your design; resize before cutting.
  • Weld overlapping shapes of the same colour so they cut as one piece.
  • Mirror iron-on (HTV) designs before cutting.
  • Avoid very thin lines — keep strokes at least about 1.5 mm thick for vinyl.
  • Add a weeding box around the design to make peeling easier.

Does this work for Silhouette too?

Yes — the same SVG works in Silhouette Studio Designer Edition and higher; the free Basic Edition cannot import SVG files. Basic Edition users can import a DXF from a vector editor such as Inkscape instead.

Can Cricut Design Space open the SVG from this converter?

Yes, upload it with Upload → Browse in Design Space. Each colour in the SVG appears as a separate layer you can assign to a different material.

Why does my design cut into lots of tiny pieces?

The source image had noise or too many colours. Lower the colour count, raise speckle removal and convert again for fewer, cleaner pieces.

How do I make a one-colour cut file?

Choose the Silhouette image type. It produces a single solid colour with a transparent background, ready for one-colour vinyl, stencils or paper.
