What is the best way to convert JPG to SVG?

The best way to convert a JPG to SVG is to trace it with a limited colour palette and speckle removal, so JPEG compression noise is merged into clean flat shapes. JPG is the most common image format, but it is also the hardest to vectorize well: its lossy compression adds faint blocks and halos around every edge, and it has no transparency, so the background is always a solid colour. This converter is tuned to deal with both.

Why does JPG need different settings from PNG?

JPG needs fewer colours and stronger speckle removal than PNG because its compression creates thousands of near-identical shades that a tracer would otherwise turn into tiny shapes. A two-colour logo saved as PNG contains a handful of colours; the same logo saved as JPG may contain several thousand. Limiting the palette is what turns that noise back into crisp edges.

Can I make a JPG background transparent?

Yes — after tracing, click the × on the background colour in the palette and those paths are removed, leaving a transparent background. Because JPG stores the background as real pixels (usually white or off-white), it becomes its own colour region during tracing. Removing it is one click, and the resulting SVG works on any background, cutting machine or website.

Which JPG images convert best?

Logos, clip art, icons, drawings and high-contrast photos convert best; low-resolution, blurry or very busy photos convert worst. For the cleanest curves, use the largest version of the JPG you can find — ideally 800 pixels or more across — and crop away anything that is not part of the artwork. If you also have a PNG version of the same image, use that instead, because it has no compression noise.

  • Great: logos, badges, icons, cartoons, signatures, line drawings.
