What does a photo look like as an SVG?

A vectorized photo becomes flat colour regions, similar to a screen print or poster, rather than a realistic copy. Vectors describe shapes with solid fills, so smooth gradients and fine textures are simplified. That is exactly why vectorized photos are popular for posters, stickers, T-shirt prints, portraits in a graphic style and wall art.

Which photos vectorize best?

  • Good: portraits with clear lighting, product shots on plain backgrounds, pets, simple landscapes and high-contrast scenes.
  • Harder: busy backgrounds, foliage, crowds, low-light and blurry photos, which create many tiny shapes.
  • Crop to the subject before converting — fewer distractions means cleaner paths.

When should you not vectorize a photo?

If you only need a sharper or smaller photo for a website, keep it as JPG, WEBP or AVIF. A detailed photo traced at high colour counts can produce a large SVG that is slower to load than the original image. Vectorize when you want the graphic style, need to scale it for print, or want to cut or engrave it. Read Photo to Vector Art for more examples.
