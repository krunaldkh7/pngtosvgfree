What is the best way to convert black and white images to SVG?

Use a two-colour trace with high detail and moderate speckle removal — this keeps thin lines intact while deleting paper grain and stray dots. Black and white artwork is the easiest type of image to vectorize, and the results are often indistinguishable from hand-drawn vector art. Sketch mode or Silhouette mode?

Sketch mode traces dark and light areas as two colours, keeping the white areas as shapes you can remove; Silhouette mode produces a single solid colour designed for cutting and engraving. Choose Sketch for artwork you will recolour or print, and Silhouette when you need one clean cut shape for vinyl, stencils or a laser.

How do I prepare a scanned drawing?

Scan at 300–600 DPI, crop tightly and increase the contrast so lines are near black and paper is pure white. Removing dust spots and pencil marks with a white brush before uploading saves clean-up later. Our guide How to Vectorize a Hand Drawing covers the full workflow.

What can I do with black and white SVGs?

  • Print line art at any size without jagged edges.
  • Cut stencils and decals with Cricut, Silhouette or a laser — see PNG to SVG for laser cutting.
  • Colour the drawing in a vector editor by filling closed areas.
  • Use signatures and hand lettering on websites, documents and merchandise.
How many colours should a black and white SVG have?

Two colours — black and white — for pure line art. Use three or four only if you want to keep grey shading.

Why are thin lines breaking up?

The lines are too faint or thin in the source. Increase contrast before uploading, raise the detail level, or use a larger scan.

Can I change the line colour?

Yes, click the black colour in the palette and choose any colour before downloading.

Will the SVG have single centre-line strokes?

No, lines are traced as filled outlines of each stroke. They look identical and can be recoloured, scaled and cut, but are not single-path strokes.
