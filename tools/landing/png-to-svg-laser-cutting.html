Why do laser cutters need SVG files?

Laser cutters follow vector paths to know exactly where to cut or score, so they need a vector file such as SVG rather than a pixel image like PNG. A PNG can be engraved as a raster image, line by line, but it cannot be cut: the machine needs outlines. Tracing the PNG into an SVG gives you closed paths for cutting and filled shapes for fast vector engraving.

How do I make sure the size is correct?

Always check and set the dimensions after importing, because programs interpret SVG pixel units differently (commonly 72 or 96 pixels per inch). In LightBurn or Glowforge, lock the aspect ratio and type the exact width in millimetres or inches before cutting.

Laser design tips

  • Respect kerf. The beam removes about 0.1–0.2 mm of material, so very thin bridges and tiny holes may burn away.
  • Keep bridges at least 1–2 mm in wood and acrylic for strength.
  • Add bridges to stencil letters such as O, A and B so inner pieces do not fall out.
  • Remove duplicate lines so the laser does not cut the same path twice.
  • Test on scrap before cutting your final material.

Which materials work with traced SVG designs?

Plywood, MDF, solid wood, cast acrylic, cardstock, leather, felt and anodised aluminium (for engraving) all work well with traced SVG designs. Wood with strong grain hides very fine detail, so keep engraved lines bolder on pine or oak. Acrylic and slate hold crisp detail and suit logos and lettering. For thin materials such as paper and felt, small details cut cleanly, but intricate designs need lower power to avoid scorching. Whatever the material, run a small test cut to dial in speed and power before the final job.

Safety first

Never laser-cut PVC or vinyl plastics, which release toxic chlorine gas. Use proper ventilation, never leave the laser unattended and keep a fire extinguisher nearby.

Does the SVG work in LightBurn?

Can I use it with Glowforge?

Yes, upload the SVG in the Glowforge app. Filled shapes can be engraved and outlines can be cut or scored.
