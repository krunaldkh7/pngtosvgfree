Can you turn a hand drawing into a vector?

Yes — a clear drawing on plain paper converts into vector lines very well. The Sketch type is tuned for dark lines on a light background: it reduces the image to a few tones and traces each stroke as a filled shape. The result keeps the character of your hand-drawn line while becoming infinitely scalable.

How do I convert a drawing to SVG?

  1. Scan or photograph the drawing

    Use even light, avoid shadows and keep the paper flat. A scanner gives the cleanest result.

  2. Upload the image

    Drop the JPG or PNG into the converter. Sketch settings are pre-selected on this page.

  3. Clean it up

    Raise Speckle removal to drop paper texture and pencil smudges; adjust Detail to keep or simplify fine strokes.

  4. Download the SVG

    Use it in Illustrator, Inkscape, Figma, Procreate, Cricut Design Space or a laser cutter.

Tips for the best drawing trace

  • Go over pencil with ink or a dark marker — faint pencil lines can disappear or break up.
  • Increase contrast in your phone's photo editor before converting if the paper looks grey.
  • Crop tightly so table edges and shadows are not traced.
  • Need one solid colour? Choose Silhouette for stencils, stamps and engraving.

What can you do with a vectorized drawing?

Common uses are logos from sketches, tattoo designs, stickers, colouring pages, embroidery and laser-engraving artwork. For a full walkthrough see How to Vectorize a Hand Drawing. Signatures are another popular use — see How to Turn Your Signature into an SVG.

Will the SVG lines be strokes or shapes?

They are filled shapes that follow the outline of each line, which preserves your line thickness variation. Most cutting and engraving software handles these directly.
