What is the difference between BMP and SVG?

BMP is an uncompressed bitmap that stores every pixel; SVG stores shapes as mathematical paths. BMP files are large and blur when enlarged. An SVG of the same artwork is usually far smaller, stays sharp at any size and can be edited in Illustrator, Inkscape, Figma or a text editor.

Where do BMP files come from?

BMP files usually come from Windows Paint, older scanners, screenshots and legacy software. Scanned logos and drawings saved as BMP are good candidates for vectorizing: use Sketch for black ink on white paper, or Logo for flat-colour artwork.

Tips for scanned bitmaps

  • Scan at 300 DPI or higher so edges have enough pixels to trace accurately.
  • Increase Speckle removal to drop dust and paper texture.
  • For a one-colour stencil or engraving file, choose Silhouette.

Will the SVG be smaller than my BMP?

Usually yes, often much smaller, because BMP stores every pixel uncompressed while SVG stores only the outlines of each colour area.
